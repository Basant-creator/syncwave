Lip-sync (all speakers vs picture)

Shifts every speaker together (+ = sound later). The sync engine aligns sound to when each frame is sent to the screen; a display's own processing delay (typically 10–30 ms on a laptop panel, more on a TV) isn't visible to the browser. Leave at 0 unless dialogue is consistently early/late on all speakers.

A/V difference: sound audible from that device minus the picture's position (smoothed, + = sound early). Compensation: measured/estimated latency correction + fine offset. Values are what each device measures about itself; physical speaker delay is only known where it was measured with the microphone.

Movie audio calibration

Automatic part: every device compensates the output latency its browser reports. Measured part: RE-CALIBRATE plays short test pulses from the laptop and the device and times them with this laptop's microphone; the result is how much later (+) or earlier the device is heard than the laptop, at the laptop. Fine offset is an optional per-device nudge (saved on the device).

Clock offset: master clock − device clock (± uncertainty). Audio latency: what the browser reports (already compensated by the scheduler). Calibration: extra shift measured acoustically (or remembered). Effective offset: total shift applied to that device's schedule.
